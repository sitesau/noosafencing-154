Match material checks to the job the fence must do

Privacy, airflow, street presentation, pool safety and acreage control point to different materials and details, so compare purpose before metres. Colorbond is described as steel panel boundary fencing in Australian colours for coastal wind exposure. Timber covers paling, lapped-and-capped and picket fences on hinterland acreage and residential blocks. Pool fencing is glass and aluminium pool barriers built to the Queensland pool safety standard. Aluminium slat suits front fences and privacy screens aligned with coastal architecture. Rural fencing includes ringlock, star-picket and strained-wire fencing. Gates include sliding, swing and solar-powered automated options for driveways and pool areas.

A practical maintenance angle is to ask what must keep performing after installation: coating condition near salt air, timber contact with soil, gate alignment, pool barrier compliance and strained wire tension on acreage runs. Those site realities change the scope more than a generic metre rate.

Neighbour and boundary process questions

A dividing fence is not only a product choice. Queensland's Neighbourhood Disputes (Dividing Fences and Trees) Act 2011 is referred to as setting a process for adjoining owners considering a sufficient dividing fence. A preferred premium material, extra height or finish is not automatically shared, and an owner who wants more than sufficient covers the difference.

For a neighbour boundary, prepare a written description of the proposed work and at least one quote. If agreement is not reached, a notice to contribute is identified as the formal step. The stated timing allows one month to reach agreement, followed by a possible QCAT application within two months of the notice. Treat that as a prompt to ask what notice, quote detail and scope evidence would be needed before work is booked, not as project advice for every dispute. Where the line is uncertain, boundary position should be checked before panels or posts are ordered.

Pool, height and sight line checks before measuring

Three pre-measure checks can change the quote. First, proposed height should be checked against the ground and any retaining wall because combined height can change the approval question. Second, a corner block can need a lower sight-line treatment so drivers can see across the intersection. Third, a pool barrier has its own safety requirements and inspection pathway, confirmed only by a licensed pool safety inspector.

For pool work, avoid asking only for a glass or aluminium price. Ask how the barrier requirement will be handled, what assumptions are being made and whether any inspection step sits outside the fence quote. The useful question is whether the quote reflects the approval, safety and visibility constraints applying to that edge of the property.

What the written quote should make explicit

A dependable quote should show a clear scope, sensible site measure, visible assumptions and written terms before work is booked. A metre rate can help with early planning, but it does not describe corners, returns, gates, hard-to-reach boundaries, old post removal, disposal or making good after removal.

  • State whether removal of the existing fence is included.
  • State the material, height and finish assumed.
  • Show how gates, returns and access constraints are treated.
  • List exclusions such as disposal, making good or inspection steps.
  • Note whether pool barrier work assumes a separate licensed inspection.

When each contractor makes inclusions explicit, the comparison turns on scope and risk rather than a headline rate.

  1. Walk the line. Record the run, suburb, height, returns, gates, access limits and old fence removal needs.
  2. Classify the boundary. Mark whether the edge is pool, neighbour, front screen, corner sight line or rural acreage.
  3. Check approval triggers. Confirm height against ground and retaining walls, plus pool barrier inspection requirements.
  4. Draft the scope. Write material, finish, inclusions, exclusions, disposal and assumptions in plain terms.
  5. Compare quotes. Match each price to the same written scope before deciding.
